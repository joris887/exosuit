# Archetype: Marketplace / Platform

**Core Question:** "What two sides are you connecting?"
**Examples:** Airbnb, Uber, Etsy, freelancer marketplaces, two-sided platforms

## Phase 2: Core Identity Questions

**Q1:** "What two sides are you connecting?"
Scaffold: "Airbnb: hosts + travelers. Uber: drivers + riders. Etsy: makers + buyers. What are YOUR two sides?"

**Q2:** "Why can't they find each other today?"
Scaffold: "Wrong channels? Lack of trust? Discovery problem? Price opacity? Geographic limitations?"

**Q3:** "Which side do you start with?"
Scaffold: "Chicken-and-egg problem. Which side is easier to attract first? How do you get them before the other side exists? Common strategies: constrain geography, seed supply yourself, start as a tool for one side."

**Q4:** "What's the transaction?"
Scaffold: "Money for services? Content exchange? Booking? What does the actual exchange look like? Is it digital or physical?"

**Q5:** "How do you build trust between strangers?"
Scaffold: "Reviews? Verification? Escrow? Insurance? Identity checks? Background checks? What makes someone trust a stranger on your platform?"

## Phase 3: Deep Dive

Walk through BOTH sides independently:

**Supply side:**
"A new seller/provider signs up. What happens? How do they create their listing/profile? What information do they provide? How long until they can start?"

**Demand side:**
"A buyer/consumer arrives. How do they discover supply? Search? Browse? Recommendation? How do they evaluate options?"

**Matching:**
"How do the two sides find each other? Algorithm? Search? Category browse? Location-based?"

**Transaction flow:**
"Step by step during the exchange. Payment? Communication? Delivery/fulfillment? What's the platform's role at each step?"

**Post-transaction:**
"After the exchange completes: Reviews? Disputes? Repeat business? How does the platform facilitate ongoing relationships?"

**Platform economics:**
Research: platform take rates, growth strategies, network effects.

## Pre-Mortem Failure Scenarios

- "Can't solve chicken-and-egg: no supply attracts no demand"
- "Users go around the platform after first transaction (disintermediation)"
- "Trust/safety issues drive users away"
- "Take rate is too high and supply side leaves"
- "Quality control is impossible at scale"
- "A well-funded competitor enters with subsidized pricing"
