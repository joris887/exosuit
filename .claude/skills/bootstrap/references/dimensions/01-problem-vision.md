# Dimension 1: Core Problem & Vision

## Introduction

"Let's start with the foundation — what problem are you solving and why it matters."

## When This Dimension Is OPEN

Ask these questions (plain English, adapt to what's already known):

1. **The problem:** "What problem does this solve? Describe it as if telling a friend who knows nothing about technology."
2. **Current alternatives:** "How are people solving this problem today? What's frustrating about the current options?"
3. **Your angle:** "What makes your approach different from what already exists?"

## Validation

A good problem statement must be:
- **Specific** — not "make things better" but "reduce the time freelancers spend creating invoices from 30 minutes to 2 minutes"
- **User-centered** — identifies who has the problem
- **Gap-aware** — explains why current solutions fall short

If the statement is vague, ask: "Can you give me a specific example? Like, walk me through what happens today when someone tries to [do the thing]."

## When This Dimension Is INFERRED

Present the inference:
"From what you've described, the core problem seems to be: **[inferred problem statement]**. Does that capture it, or would you refine it?"

## Output

Record:
- **Problem statement:** One clear sentence
- **Target audience:** Who has this problem
- **Current alternative:** How it's handled today
- **Unique value:** What makes this solution different
- **Rationale:** Why now — what changed that makes this solvable/needed

## Recommendation Logic

No recommendation needed — the user is the authority on their own problem. The framework's role is to sharpen the statement, not choose it.
